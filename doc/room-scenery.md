# Gameplay table and stationary backdrop

Owner decisions, 2026-11-15; design #1367 and chart audition #1363. This target supersedes the earlier shared-camera chart placement in [Line Goes Up](line-goes-up.md). No runtime migration or tuned production settings are claimed here.

## Independent composition and visible behavior

A **table capability** places the real baize/rim geometry beneath gameplay blocks. It receives their shadows, uses the gameplay camera/projection and therefore zooms with them. It is not a child of the rotating block assembly: rotating cards does not turn the table. Existing center/radius following and shadow-quality behavior are preserved as configured presentation policies during extraction; the owner has not requested a new table-follow algorithm or gameplay pivot.

A **backdrop capability** supplies stationary room imagery and a background-decoration host with projection independent of gameplay zoom, automatic field framing, field rotation and table-follow movement. A procedural bitmap is an acceptable backdrop; a physical wall mesh is not required. Resizing/aspect/safe-area changes recompute its configured anchors; those are layout changes, not gameplay camera movement.

The framed score chart is a real 3D picture in that stationary backdrop host, initially anchored toward the **top-right** with configured margins, apparent dimensions and readable rest orientation. Its screen position and apparent size do not change when the player zooms or when a growing field reframes. Keep the existing plot, actual 3D frame, named attachment pivots and ordered release/fall/audio; this is not an HTML chart or flattening of the frame into the wall bitmap. Owner tuning happens in the bench before a separate production-config adoption.

Modes/scenes compose table, backdrop and decorations through registered traits and configured services. They can omit the table or supply another picture without generic services knowing their identity. Existing `tableBackground` composition is migration evidence, not a permanent catch-all scene flag. A behavior trait installs its mount/pass contributions; renderers dispatch registrations, never test mode/scene/graph IDs or trait presence as flags.

**Selected target:** a reusable background-decoration host with an orthographic layout camera and its own scene/root. Its viewport/aspect mapping derives from the visible game surface. Configuration provides normalized anchor, offsets/margins, apparent width/height or scale, frame orientation and depth within this host. Fixed orthographic orientation still permits a 3D frame and swinging perspective cues from its geometry/rotation. It never reads gameplay zoom or compensates against a card's position. Anchor constraints keep the complete framed bounds within the usable background area where possible; authoring controls expose compact/narrow layout overrides instead of silently clipping labels.

This uses the existing codebase's separate-camera/owned-frame patterns. A physical wall would add geometry and still require a separate camera to prevent zoom; a graph-only inverse zoom transform would couple one prop to gameplay framing and fail reuse. A DOM screenshot pasted onto the backdrop would lose the 3D attachment effect. A dedicated WebGL renderer for each prop would duplicate contexts/lifecycle and is unnecessary. These are source-backed design alternatives, not measured performance comparisons. No browser experiment of this target projection has yet been performed.

## Render ordering, shadows and occlusion

Reuse the current two canvases and shared host resources. The back canvas's ordered composition is: stationary backdrop imagery, background-decoration scene, then table/gameplay-background pass. Each declared contribution has an explicit clear/depth policy; the backdrop is cleared once, later stages preserve color and use their own depth domain as needed. The table's scene background must not erase the earlier background result. The hand/board/drag front canvas and DOM layering retain their existing ownership.

The table is drawn over background decorations using the gameplay camera after those decorations are drawn. This **screen composition order**, not sharing depth values from unrelated cameras, makes the picture fall behind the visible table silhouette. Do not compare backdrop orthographic depth against gameplay perspective depth or restore a stale scene.background. Card shadow casters still feed the same table shadow map; their shadow-only stand-ins do not draw a dark rectangle over the backdrop. A table occludes only its actual visible geometry, not an invisible full-screen panel. Front cards continue to cover both under existing composition.

The picture's swing and fall remain in its backdrop parent's local units. On resize, recompute the host projection/anchor without resetting effect progress; capture its start pose and preserve continuity. Its path must pass behind the table's actual projected coverage before its finite completion. The initial bench can tune fall distance/path and image location using the real table silhouette. Do not hide the prop early to fake a missing fall. Completion still emits exactly one impact cue; cancellation produces none. Picture effects do not become a new outcome/input gate. The configured 80%-burn loss rule remains independent.

Registered render contributions own their scene/root, camera, mount and disposal, while the render host owns contexts and pass execution. Save/restore render target, viewport/scissor, clear state, camera layers and temporarily changed materials in finally-style cleanup even when a pass fails. Registration/removal and nested scene cover/resume use handles/reference ownership, not scene-name counters or global mutable identity maps. Ordinary shader/optics prepasses receive the composed backdrop/table inputs they need from their host; declarations cannot silently capture another scene's stale output. Existing screenshot composition captures the final back canvas, so it need not invent a chart-specific layer.
