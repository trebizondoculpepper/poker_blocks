# Calm Water audition refinements (#794)

This is a provisional Transparent Materials Bench experiment, not production Water approval. Follow-ups #795–#797 implement focused controls, appearance and shading slices. Preserve #675's active owner and existing prototype; no renderer replacement is authorized here.

## Period, edges and preset compatibility

Calm Water owns period bounds 0.5–8 seconds, step 0.5, default 8 seconds. One period is one complete cycle, not Hz. Store bounds on the configured preset capability and consume the same bounds in validation, controls and clock advancement; other presets retain their present bounds/defaults. Reject malformed, nonfinite or nonpositive periods. A well-formed imported old Calm Water period of 10 seconds clamps to 8 through the generic preset-bound normalizer, with an explicit adjustment notice. Copy settings exports the effective value; do not silently display 0.5 while the clock still runs at 8. Compatible schemaVersion 1 input remains importable; new optional parameters take documented defaults.

Drawn outlines are independent of Fresnel reflection. Expose appearance edge opacity through the common preset configuration, controls and settings import/export, range 0–1. Initial Calm Water edge opacity is 0.08 (reversible audition default); zero removes drawn outlines completely. Keep reflection and other presets unchanged. The surface can still show grazing reflection when outlines are disabled.

## Selected surface-ripple shading experiment

The existing custom GLSL uses normalize(vWorldNormal) for incidence, refraction offset, reflection and Fresnel, while rippleShift(vLocal) changes only captured-background lookup. A standard material bumpMap property cannot affect that shader automatically. Select a small procedural height gradient in object-local space, evaluated from fixed smooth sinusoidal waves sharing the existing ripple phase, period and spatial scale. Project the gradient into the local face tangent plane, perturb the local normal, transform by the correct inverse-transpose normal transform to world space, then normalize. This preserves smooth behavior under rotation and nonuniform scale; mat3(modelMatrix) alone is not generally a valid normal transform.

Use the same perturbed world normal consistently in the rear and front optical passes wherever incidence, refraction offset, reflection or Fresnel use a surface normal. Keep geometric front/back culling and silhouette unchanged. Do not perturb an already world-space normal using untransformed local gradients. Retain flat facet normals as the baseline; do not subdivide facets or introduce a normal seam from screen-space derivatives.

Expose independent surfaceRippleStrength, range 0–0.08, provisional default 0.015, as dimensionless gradient strength. At zero, use the previous shader normal path exactly, preserving the baseline. Lookup amplitude remains independent, including the supplied 0.006. Phase uses the existing per-preset presentation clock; reduced motion holds it. No new wall clock, random noise, texture allocation or scene capture pass. Copy settings includes effective strength, edge opacity and period, never uniforms, textures or derived phase. Fixed analytic waves avoid a texture asset and allow continuity; a sampled normal map would require an extra asset, filtering/seam policy and lifecycle, so defer it unless visual evidence shows the analytic approach inadequate. Derivatives of a height field are a candidate, but explicit analytic gradients keep the coordinate convention inspectable without screen-space derivative dependencies.
