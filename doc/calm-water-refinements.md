# Calm Water audition refinements (#794)

This is a provisional Transparent Materials Bench experiment, not production Water approval. Follow-ups #795–#797 implement focused controls, appearance and shading slices. Preserve #675's active owner and existing prototype; no renderer replacement is authorized here.

## Period, edges and preset compatibility

Calm Water owns period bounds 0.5–8 seconds, step 0.5, default 8 seconds. One period is one complete cycle, not Hz. Store bounds on the configured preset capability and consume the same bounds in validation, controls and clock advancement; other presets retain their present bounds/defaults. Reject malformed, nonfinite or nonpositive periods. A well-formed imported old Calm Water period of 10 seconds clamps to 8 through the generic preset-bound normalizer, with an explicit adjustment notice. Copy settings exports the effective value; do not silently display 0.5 while the clock still runs at 8. Compatible schemaVersion 1 input remains importable; new optional parameters take documented defaults.

Drawn outlines are independent of Fresnel reflection. Expose appearance edge opacity through the common preset configuration, controls and settings import/export, range 0–1. Initial Calm Water edge opacity is 0.08 (reversible audition default); zero removes drawn outlines completely. Keep reflection and other presets unchanged. The surface can still show grazing reflection when outlines are disabled.

## Rotation-triggered concentric highlight ripples (owner revision #1000)

The owner prefers Calm Water over the outgoing contained water appearance, but could not see a useful surface ripple in the first continuous-wave trial. Supersede that trial: concentric circular ripples are centred on each planar face, excited by rotation, then decay for a few seconds to flat at rest. This perturbs reflection/specular highlights only. Preserve the existing animated internal refraction, base refraction normal/offset, magnification, dispersion, clarity and Fresnel weighting. Geometry, lettering, culling and silhouette remain unchanged. A shader normal shared across all optical calculations would violate this separation.

A registered appearance capability owns local face coordinates/bounds and a deterministic radial height gradient. Project its gradient into the face tangent plane and use the inverse-transpose local-to-world normal transform for the highlight normal. Handle the face centre without dividing by zero; sample each face about its own centre, not the cube centroid projected incorrectly. The motion adapter supplies object rotation deltas; camera orbit alone must not trigger it. Accumulate bounded excitation from angular motion; repeated turns cannot grow amplitude without bound. Share immutable definitions, but keep each displayed object's excitation/decay state independent. No preset/material/room identity tests in the renderer.

Reversible audition defaults: bounded amplitude uses existing surfaceRippleStrength (0–0.08); a release-to-rest duration of 45 game-time units (3 seconds) and two outward rings across a half-face are initial hypotheses, configurable on the capability. A smooth bounded envelope reaches exact zero at the configured rest duration. Use the host's one presentation clock, not wall-clock deadlines or frame counts. This ambient per-frame shading runs through the appearance update path, not thousands of scheduled events. Reduced motion suppresses rotation excitation and holds the surface flat; internal refraction keeps its separately documented reduced-motion policy. Reset/disposal clears excitation. No additional capture pass, facet subdivision, runtime noise allocation or texture asset is required by this selected analytic approach.

Copy Settings exports effective authored controls, not current phase, transforms, textures or excitation. Older configurations missing new decay/frequency values take declared defaults with an explicit normalization notice where appropriate. Do not overwrite the owner's attached fixture while adding controls. Verify strength zero and rest baseline, multiple faces, grazing light, nonuniform scale, independent objects, repeated rotation, reduced motion, reset and repeated backend switching. Include a fixed-light rotation/release clip that actually shows concentric highlights; a passing gradient test alone is not visual acceptance.
