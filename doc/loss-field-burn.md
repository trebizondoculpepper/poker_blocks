# Terminal loss: staggered playfield burn (#790)

Owner-approved presentation: only the terminal playfield cards burn, in a cosmetic random order. Music winds down when this burn starts. Game Over appears after approximately 80% have completed, and remaining burns continue behind it. Hand, deck, rack, stock and menus do not burn. This is visual disposal of captured terminal-field ghosts, not gameplay destruction.

## Ordering and scope ownership

First finalize the loss through the model lifecycle, including any registered outcome-proposal modifiers. Finish all required deciding-action scoring and material narration, then play its configured loss-sequence cue, as in [outcome presentation](outcome-presentation.md). Do not hide required action narration to reach the burn threshold earlier.

After that action scope settles, a composed terminal-field presentation controller starts the burn sequence and sends the configured windDown operation to the music controller. It owns a captured outgoing-field presentation root independent of the incoming Game Over menu root. Generic navigation/services only consume configured completion references and commands, never an outcome or scene-name branch.

For N captured field cards, the result gate settles once ceil(0.8*N) distinct burn effects finish. N=0 settles immediately, N=1 waits for that one. Count completions, not starts. Partition required burn effects and optional tail before scheduling: the first ceil(0.8*N) effects in captured order belong to the gate's required scope; the remaining tail belongs to a sibling lease, never a required child that accidentally keeps the gate pending. This separate exit-presentation gate follows the completed action scope; it does not relabel required scoring/material feedback as optional. The configured Game Over scene then enters with its own entrance cue. Do not wait for the music fade or either sound sample to finish.

Use a cosmetic shuffle at sequence creation, captured once independently of session RNG. Initial reversible timing is a 6-game-time-unit burn duration per card and a total start spread of at most 12 units: start i at i*min(1,12/max(1,N-1)) units. This bounds starts for large fields while preserving overlap. Use the one shared scheduler/presentation clock; no per-card timers or view-owned queue. Existing ambient shader animation stays frame-driven. Each effect settles once; reduced motion shows the captured final summary and completes the finite burn scopes promptly without replaying sounds. A rendering failure uses bounded summary completion rather than leaving the result gate pending forever.

Tail leases survive the Game Over foreground mounting so the captured field can finish behind it. New Run/Main Menu, restart, superseding generation or destination teardown cancels the entire outgoing root, scheduled work and all tail effects before the next board becomes visible. Ordinary cover by the result menu must not dispose that root early. Keep accessible menu targets responsive; no generic input-busy lock. Cancelled/superseded effects cannot later open an obsolete result screen or settle another generation's scope.

## Model, audio and evidence

The model already owns its final score, outcome, save state and card locations. Burn callbacks cannot remove cards, emit block.destroyed, consume RNG, invoke material effects or issue rewards. Do not reuse a destructive model command merely to obtain its artwork. A configured transition/burn sound may accompany the sequence; ordinary destruction success cues are not authorized by this visual cleanup. Reuse registered IDs/placeholders and one response owner per occurrence.
