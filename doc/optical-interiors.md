# Captured optical interiors and shell-free bubbles (#1225)

This supplies the missing interior/pass contract for #676, using the accepted [Calm Water composition](calm-water-refinements.md). It is a bench prototype, not production promotion (#1015). Preserve the shared optics and independent highlight work; no new material rules, physics, mass or gameplay randomness. Implementation is separate: interior capture/composite, full-volume provider, pooled-bubble emission/growth, then the bench composition.

## Appearance-owned interior capability

Reuse `InteriorContribution` as the integration boundary: the optical appearance publishes a registered provider to which an inclusion contributes its explicit draw handle and volume mount. The provider owns capture routing; the bubble effect owns its pool, positions and cosmetic update. Extend registration to return an idempotent release handle, with the existing contained transmission provider adapted through that same lifecycle. It is not a material-name test or a generic `isWater` flag. Registration may not mutate shared particle materials invisibly or leave a previous host's layer/capture binding on a reused appearance.

An optical interior registration includes an opaque renderer-context/mount generation, declared volume clipping capability, transform, and explicitly leased visual resources. The host composes the same capability on a different volume without adding renderer identity branches. Registration/disposal is per displayed appearance, not per material definition. A visual packet can reference current visual buffers for frame rendering, but no delayed handler may read model state or use session RNG. Rotation, per-frame contents and shader clocks stay on the existing ambient frame clock.

Calm Water's opaque front remains the composite owner: interior color is resolved with its transmitted background before outer reflection and printed artwork. Inclusion particles are not drawn a second time as a main-scene overlay. Do not globally disable depth tests or increase transparency merely to expose them.

## Bounded capture proposal

Use renderer-owned **per-volume tiles in shared interior color/depth targets**, rather than one full-scene render target/pass per block. A tile contains only that registration's interior contribution, rendered at its captured world transform through the host camera; it cannot contain another volume's particles. Shared background capture continues to hide the collection. Add a sampled depth attachment to that already-rendered background target, without a second full-scene depth render. Keep the existing rear-offset pass. The new pass draws only registered interior proxies into their owned tiles, then the main optical front samples its own tile through an explicitly bound tile transform.

The initial bounded profile is 128×128 pixels per tile, configurable 64–256, at most 64 simultaneous registrations and at most 2048 pixels per atlas edge; effective limits also respect renderer capabilities. These are reversible prototype budgets, not measured performance. Exceeding capacity, unavailable depth/targets or allocation/context failure produces an explicit development diagnostic/unavailable prototype response and cleanup; it never silently overlays particles, samples another tile or makes a production gameplay decision. Zero-emission plain water registers no interior draw/tile. A registration's tile handle includes generation, so a late appearance cannot sample a recycled slot. No per-frame target allocation.

Render each tile with preserved target, viewport/scissor, camera projection/layers, background, clear state and visibility via the shared preservation capability. The tile mapping must preserve the same screen projection used by the front, including drawing-buffer resolution, camera changes and non-square viewports. A small tile is a reduced-resolution image, not a separately positioned camera whose parallax differs. Proxies share explicitly leased geometry/updated particle buffers and own any capture-specific material binding; never reparent or dispose the live inclusion as a side effect of capture. Tile draws must use only the registered contribution, not `renderer.render` over the full scene once for every block.

Depth values are compared in one documented linear view-depth convention after converting the background's hardware depth. For each sample, validate that its interior depth belongs to the declared volume's front/back ray interval and lies before the captured opaque-scene occluder at that lookup coordinate. Discard invalid samples and use the background contribution. A contribution's declared clipping adapter enforces its local wet volume and sprite bounds, rather than letting a point's center be inside while the whole billboard spills outside. The initial provider is the configured convex cube volume; a different volume needs a compatible registered clipping provider, not a renderer shape-name test.

Compose premultiplied interior color over the sampled transmitted background using its captured coverage, then apply the existing clarity/dispersion/Fresnel/reflection and print ordering. Interior and background use corresponding optical lookup coordinates; a missing/invalid interior sample leaves the original background unchanged. Preserve the captured particle's soft rim without treating its invisible texels as opaque depth. Multiple overlapping particles need an explicit depth/coverage rule in the provider (initially nearest nonzero covered sample); do not claim physically correct multilayer scattering. Representative soft-edge/depth evidence is required before accepting that approximation.

Per-volume tiles prevent cross-volume particle leakage. Opaque main-scene objects and the front still use ordinary depth, and the background depth test prevents occluded interior samples shining through an opaque neighbor. This does not add mutual refraction between transparent blocks or ray-traced caustics: the existing nearest-rear-offset limitation remains. Overlapping transparent volumes must be auditioned and reported as a known approximation, not hidden by a depth-disabled overlay. If this bounded design cannot satisfy the stated visible-interior/depth checks, report the exact failed scene before proposing a larger optical architecture.

## Full-volume and pooled-bubble composition

A registered shell-free volume provider publishes the existing interior boundary and wet-membership contract without creating a plastic shell. It supplies the block-local clipping bounds, liquid top and world-up rise direction as explicit capabilities. Plane normal and motion direction must not be conflated under nonuniform scale: local plane normal derives from the world-up covector, while rise direction derives from inverse-transforming the world-up vector. Reuse the existing provider for uniform contained volumes through the same interface; do not add a material or container branch. Test tilted and nonuniform transforms or explicitly reject unsupported transforms with a diagnostic, never silently claim world-up motion.

Compose the existing pooled `risingBubbles` inclusion against that provider. Add emission frequency independent of fixed pool capacity, and per-particle start/end size interpolated over normalized ascent progress. Reuse its birth/wet checks, wall margin, wobble/rest and cosmetic randomness rather than another particle simulation. The wall margin uses maximum configured bubble extent; birth must fit. Retirement follows the declared surface/bounds and clamps progress. Zero frequency emits none and avoids unnecessary live resources; the zero-allocation composition omits the inclusion through its configured handler. Reduced motion freezes the declared appearance consistently with existing bubbling behavior.

Water, Hot Water and Boiling Water are configuration presets over one appearance/provider/inclusion path. The bench supplies zero/low/higher emission, hotter larger average size, small-at-birth growth and a deliberately restrained boiling density. Expose pool count, emission rate, start/end size, rise, wobble, rest, color and opacity independently from optical/highlight controls. Configuration units and ranges live on their registered schemas; export effective settings through the shared Copy Settings path. Current particle positions/lifetime/atlas handles are transient, not serialized authored settings. Production consumers/retirement of old water visuals remain #1015 after this prototype is useful.

## Evidence and delivery limits

Source audit at `4596084`: `src/visual/contained.ts:InteriorContribution` already exposes `include(Points)` and `LiquidSurface` supplies local normal/height/wet. `src/visual/filled-vessel.ts:transmissionCapture` makes particles opaque/alpha-tested so Three.js transmission can capture them, proving an existing optical integration seam; it does not solve Calm Water, whose `src/visual/crystal-refraction.ts:createRefractionCapture` hides the entire collection during background capture and whose front shader outputs alpha 1. `rising-bubbles.ts` already calls the interior provider and owns the pool, but its size is constant and count is not a rate. These are concrete reuse/extension points, not evidence the new atlas or depth validation exists.

Drawing child particles normally is hidden by the opaque front; transparent overlays lose depth/volume isolation. Putting all interiors in one undifferentiated background image cannot identify the sampled volume and risks neighbor leakage. Per-block full-scene captures multiply scene cost. Isolated contribution tiles share target allocation and capture only the small explicit draw packets, at the cost of additional tile draws and reduced-resolution sampling. No pass-count or GPU improvement is claimed without measurement. Reusing Three.js physical transmission would replace the chosen optical model and is outside this contract.

Verify top/side visibility, rotated/nonuniform volumes, near/far and opaque/transparent overlaps, neighboring differently colored interiors, printed artwork/reflection order, sprite rims/bounds and actual world-up rise. Include resize, high DPI, context loss/recovery, capacity failure, tile reuse, two hosts sharing an asset, backend switching, reduced motion and exact release/resource counts. Profile 1/10/50 volumes with **all** background/rear/interior/main passes, tile draws, clears, allocations, resolution and CPU/GPU limits reported; no requirement that an extra tile draw is a full-scene pass. Provide stationary/rotation clips with revision/settings. Source-only audit and pure math tests do not establish the bench's visual acceptance.
