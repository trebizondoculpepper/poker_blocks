# Line Goes Up (#1062, design #1082)

Owner-approved target; runtime work is split into #1063–#1066, #1080 and #1085–#1087. This document defines reusable capabilities, not a graph baked into the background. The graph is informative room scenery; it never awards points, predicts authoritative outcomes or blocks player input.

## Stage history is model data

At stage initialization create a stage-scoped immutable score-history value with the initial `(play: 0, total: 0)` point, the stage's configured play allowance and target, and an opaque stage generation. The allowance comes from resolved `StageRules.plays`, never six or a mode lookup. Each successfully committed scoring play appends exactly one entry containing action correlation, one-based play index, awarded delta and cumulative stage total. A zero-point play still appends. Discards, refusals, preview scoring, loading and presentation callbacks append nothing. Stage reset replaces this history; shopping does not synthesize a scoring entry.

The scoring model's registered commit operation owns appending alongside the actual total, within the same transaction/state rollback boundary. Presentation controllers cannot append. The committed action exports the captured history revision/point and stage generation for consumers. The append must survive even when no graph is composed; do not implement a view subscription that retrospectively mutates the model. Exactly-once action correlation prevents duplicate publication from appending twice. Retain history through the terminal stage result until the next stage initializes it.

Persist this plain history through the normal versioned save codec, validate finite nonnegative values, ordered unique play indexes/action correlations and cumulative/delta consistency, and restore without replaying scoring, rewards or audio. Future variable-allowance rules must publish their resolved bound to the same state rather than making the view infer it. An invalid history fails validation explicitly under the existing save recovery contract.

**Legacy-save migration assumption:** old saves cannot reconstruct earlier per-play scores. Preserve their authoritative current score and used-play checkpoint, mark earlier samples unavailable, and append future committed plays from there. Show the known checkpoint without inventing intermediate points or drawing a supposedly observed path across missing history. New stages have complete history from zero. Resolve used-play count from persisted/resolved stage rules; report unavailable rather than guessing if an older/custom mode cannot provide it. This narrowly scoped compatibility limit does not authorize resetting an existing stage or altering its score. Add migration coverage before enabling the codec change.

## Four independently composable presentation capabilities

### Plot surface (#1085)

A reusable 2D canvas plot component accepts plain plot data: explicit axis bounds/tick labels, observed series, reference lines and optional projection series, plus styling and pixel-resolution configuration. It exposes its canvas/render-surface output, revision/update operation and disposal. It knows no `GameRun`, frame, background, target policy or mode. A texture adapter consumes changed revisions and updates its owned texture; do not allocate a new GPU texture every frame. Surface/texture ownership must be explicit so shared surfaces are not disposed by an arbitrary consumer. Context or allocation failure returns an unavailable surface without affecting gameplay.

The score projection/axis adapter supplies the score-specific data below; the plot component merely draws it. It must also support a non-score series as evidence that there is no embedded score policy.

### Framed picture and scenery placement (#1086)

A framed-picture capability accepts an arbitrary picture surface/texture and owns its frame geometry and surface mesh. The composing scene declares its room-root parent, position, orientation, size, frame appearance and optional attachment/effect configuration. Position is data on the scene's composition; neither the renderer nor `createCardRoom` contains a hard-coded graph location or identity test. A second framed picture can use another surface and transform without changing the service. Expose attachment points in object-local coordinates for effects; these are geometry, not stage policy.

The initial graph placement is toward the top-right on the room's apparent back wall, readable under the normal play camera and outside the main cards' resting silhouette. It is fixed in room space, not parented to the rotating card assembly or pinned to screen pixels. Camera movement changes its perspective naturally; cards/table may occlude it. The room currently uses a procedural backdrop texture rather than a modeled wall: compose a world-space picture at the configured room transform without claiming an existing wall mesh or adding a graph-specific branch to backdrop generation. Verify and tune that transform in the scene definition at wide/narrow aspect ratios; no camera or mass-weighted pivot redesign is included.

### Committed-score presentation controller (#1087)

Use the existing committed-action channel, with immutable facts and explicit captured snapshots. A configured presentation controller trait subscribes for its scene lifetime, selects the registered score-history projection from committed scoring records, and requests plot updates through services. The generic game controller wires this trait and the channel but has no graph logic. `score.totalApplied` is evidence that the model has finalized the play delta, not permission for a delayed callback to read live `GameRun` state. If the committed export lacks the captured history payload, #1063 adds that reusable projection to the commit record; do not introduce a second event bus or diff score/moves to infer an action.

Use the [action-correlated phase reservation interface](action-presentation-phases.md) for this ordering. The graph reserves its required post-score contribution at commit before the action root seals; existing scoring producers must expose per-action completion first. No private presenter lookup or global narrator-idle shortcut.

On entry/restoration draw the captured committed history immediately without replay. For a new committed play, present its new observed segment/point in the action's **post-scoring** presentation scope, after that action's pre-score/scoring feedback. Configure initial growth duration as 4 game-time units (4/15 s), a reversible presentation default. Queue immutable revisions in action order on the one scheduler; a later play remains legal. A committed stage reset cancels the old generation so late graph effects cannot repaint the next stage. Exit unsubscribes and cancels owned scopes; redraw on re-entry comes from state. Failure falls only after the failed action's required graph update, without making unrelated ambient effects into result gates.

### Triggered attachment-release effect (#1065)

A separately registered effect attaches to any compatible framed object, accepts captured object/attachment transforms and configured timings, and is triggered by a controller's request. It knows neither graph nor stage identity. The loss-presentation controller composes the request in response to the committed failure record, after the final plot update. It does not put loss policy in the picture or renderer.

Sequence: release the **left** attachment; rotate downward about the remaining **right** attachment; release the right; fall behind the table. Preserve world pose when changing pivots/parents. Initial tuning is 6 units for the hanging swing, then 8 units for the fall; the second release follows the first swing. Make these configured units, amplitudes and gravity/easing parameters on the effect, not timers or frame constants. The ordinary shared scheduler/clock advances the finite effect. Geometry depth/table occlusion hides the fall; do not remove the picture early merely because a result overlay appears. On its finite fall completion emit a captured named impact/completion cue exactly once. Cancellation settles once and emits no later impact. Repeated triggers in the same generation do not duplicate the sequence.

The separate sound controller (#1080) consumes that cue and schedules a suitable smashing-glass sample after an initial 3-unit delay. Register/reuse a dotted SFX id and supply actual committed audio under the sound-design workflow; a placeholder is an interim fallback, not final audio acceptance. Sound never decides loss or manipulates picture transforms. Reduced motion skips the swing/fall to a hidden settled picture and supplies one bounded completion cue; the delayed audio still occurs once unless scene teardown cancels it. Muting follows the shared sound settings.

Coordinate with #946's playfield-only randomized loss burns/music wind-down and 80%-burn result reveal. Preserve that reveal rule: picture animation/audio do not extend the required burn threshold or delay the model outcome. Keep the effect on the surviving room/result host through ordinary result-overlay reveal; dispose it on actual room teardown. Music and burn remain independent configured controllers, not branches in the picture effect.

## Score-specific graph configuration and projection (#1064, #1066)

- Plot cumulative **stage** score against scoring-play index. A new stage starts at `(0,0)` and X spans zero through the entire configured allowance from the outset. Discards do not change X or add samples.
- Draw an identifiable dotted horizontal reference at the current target. Initially `yMax = target / 0.8`, placing it 80% up the graph. Expand upward when necessary, retaining headroom with `yMax = max(target, maximumObservedTotal) / 0.8`. Never crop the winning overshoot; the existing rules still end the stage as soon as it wins. Bounds do not shrink during a stage. Require a positive finite target or show an explicit unavailable-target state; never divide by zero.
- After at least one scoring play, draw a visually distinct straight **average pace** line from origin through the latest actual sample and extend it to the X-axis limit. Its slope is `total / playsUsed`, not the latest play delta. Where slope is positive, the continuous target-crossing estimate is `target / slope`; the first whole play reaching it is its ceiling. A zero total has no finite crossing estimate. Never display an invented crossing before a first play.
- Keep the full allowed-play X range fixed even when projected crossing lies beyond it. Show a boundary annotation such as “beyond available plays” rather than silently expanding the X-axis or predicting a win. Clip projection to plot bounds so a steep pace does not force unlimited Y expansion; observed scores determine Y expansion. Fractional crossing is information, not a new game rule.
- Preserve text readability, distinguish observed/projection/target beyond color alone, and provide a semantic text summary of score, target, plays used/allowed and projected crossing when available. The scenery is noninteractive, never a draggable/drop target or focus trap. Hidden semantic text is compatible with the world-space presentation.

## Evidence, decisions and verification limits

Audited source seams: `src/game-run.ts:resolvePlay` applies `scoreBefore + total.value`, emits `score.totalApplied`, resolves after-score steps and settles outcome; `src/round.ts:Round` has score/target/moves but no history, while `src/game-state.ts:StageRules` owns `plays`. `src/save.ts` encodes round numbers, so history requires a codec addition rather than a private view cache. `src/experiment-recorder.ts:RecorderSource` demonstrates `onCommitted` and captured fact recording; its intentionally empty-on-restore experimental history is not production stage persistence. `src/play-presenter.ts` registers a shared-scheduler action; its legacy private paths are not permission for another queue. `src/visual/card-room.ts:createCardRoom/roomBackdrop` builds the moving table/lamp and texture background, not a configurable picture host. None of these observations claims the new interfaces already exist.

A HUD canvas would be simpler but conflicts with the requested physical prop. A frame that polls `GameRun` would couple services to gameplay and lose transaction/history semantics. A monolithic graph/frame/loss object would prevent reuse. Separate plain-data plot, picture, controller and release-effect capabilities satisfy both reuse and lifecycle ownership. Proposed units, styles and initial room transform are reversible tuning; the accepted behavior and save semantics are the contract.

Verification must include: mode play limits other than six; zero-score and overshooting plays; discards/refusals unchanged; stage reset; save/restore and missing legacy history without fabricated samples; rollback/exactly-once history; later input while old feedback plays; immutable captured payloads; cancellation and stale generation; target 80% and Y expansion; average-rate versus last-delta projection; zero and beyond-budget crossing; alternate picture positions and a non-graph surface; world occlusion, narrow/wide readability and semantic summary; correct right pivot and ordered releases; hidden fall then delayed audible glass once; ordinary result-overlay survival versus actual teardown; reduced motion and repeated entry/exit. Supply screenshots and a clip with revision/settings. This docs-only contract supplies no browser/performance acceptance evidence for the future implementation.
