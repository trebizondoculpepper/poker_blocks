# Action-correlated presentation phases (#1216)

The [Line Goes Up controller](line-goes-up.md#committed-score-presentation-controller-1087) needs an independent post-score contribution. It must not inspect private presenter queues or start its growth at commit time. Extend the existing committed-action presentation composition with opaque action-correlated phase reservations, using the one visual scheduler. This is a missing presentation interface, not a new gameplay bus or scheduler.

## One action record, reserved contributions

A composed action-presentation controller owns an action record keyed by captured run generation plus opaque model `actionId`. Its registry service stores handles/status only, never gameplay policy or a second due-time queue. Committed facts are its input; services receive captured payloads and handles, never `GameRun`. Registered producer capabilities declare their phase and deterministic composition order before the scene subscribes. Registration is by capability/handler reference, not scene, action kind or material identity. A producer may return no contribution for a captured record through its registered handler; generic dispatch never tests a fact/trait as an enabled flag.

On successful committed-action delivery, the controller synchronously snapshots the active producer registrations and asks them to reserve their contributions. Before any root is sealed or any completion is published, create its required root and child contribution leases. The graph producer reserves its post-score contribution now, even though its growth runs later. The scoring producer reserves its pre-score/landing and scoring contributions using the same action key. The existing presenter adapter binds its flight/narration work to those reserved handles rather than inventing a second local action identity. Registration order cannot let one producer finish/seal the action before another reserves its required child.

The collection closes once all snapshotted producers have returned their reservations; it is not an open-ended opportunity for observers to add required work after completion. A late producer or unknown/stale action lookup gets an explicit unavailable result. It can draw current scenery immediately on entry, but cannot resurrect an old action or prolong an already settled result. No replayed graph growth on restore. Existing in-flight records belong to the registration snapshot even if later registrations change.

Each reservation is an opaque contribution handle with a captured action/generation, phase and terminal status. It can bind its actual scheduler work once, settle without effects for an empty contribution, or cancel/fail once. The phase controller exposes read-only status/completion observation by action key plus phase/contribution handle; observers cannot inspect or mutate another producer's private resources. Controller traits receive only the handle capabilities they own. IDs correlate records and deduplicate; they never select behavior.

## Phase barriers and ordering

The action's presentation phases are configured protocol slots: pre-scoring, scoring, post-scoring. They are not gameplay phase switches in a generic controller. A registered phase-plan capability declares the barrier dependencies and uses the same scheduler's scopes/effects to enforce them. No `setTimeout`, deadline against wall time, parallel due-time heap or new presenter-owned timed queue. A reserved gated contribution is owned required work from the outset, so a sealed parent cannot settle while waiting for a phase to open.

Pre-scoring settles when the action's required placement/opening/material work settles. Scoring settles when that action's required line narration and its final required tint/material tail settle. Opening post-scoring uses **that action's** scoring completion, not global narrator idle, latest score, an outcome cue or just the placement-flight scope. A zero-line/zero-score action still has an explicit scoring completion after its applicable prerequisites. The graph's four-unit segment starts at this post-score anchor using its captured history point.

A producer submits its finite scheduled work under its reserved child scope. Existing work created before adoption is joined through an explicit completion adapter; it does not become evidence that a parent flight scope includes narration. Scoring success and bounded summary/failure both release the phase through registered fallback policy. Reset cancellation does not masquerade as successful scoring completion and cannot open a stale graph phase or result. The service's status observation distinguishes finished, cancelled and failed; consumers must use their documented failure/summary response rather than treat any settlement as success.

Per-action keying alone does not serialize a shared plot. The configured ordered-contribution capability links a plot's post-score contribution to the previous captured action's plot completion in committed publication order. It stores predecessor handles, not due times or live state. Independent finite effects may still overlap where the documented plan allows. Preserve the existing narrator's committed line order; completing an earlier action's graph must not wait for all later actions to narrate. Independent targets need not share an ordering chain. Cancellation removes that generation's chain; the next stage begins from its captured zero state.

Only the action's full required root, including already reserved post-score children, is eligible to release its outcome-completion response. The loss cue and subsequent burn transition consume that completion; they must not fire at the earlier scoring anchor. The graph cannot create a deadlock by waiting for that outcome cue while the cue waits for the graph. Result/ambient tail leases that the existing contracts mark optional remain outside this root. Picture fall/audio and the 80% burn gate retain their separately documented ordering and do not become required scoring children.

## Bounded lifecycle and fallback

A producer may need to reserve at commit before the existing presenter binds its visual work after the model call returns. That binding is synchronous in the same input action when possible; never defer it to a private timer. Unbound reservations are not allowed to hold unsealed scopes forever: represent the wait as bounded work on the shared scheduler, with configured game-time-unit limit, diagnostic and captured summary fallback. Initial ceiling is 450 units (the existing 30-second scheduler limit), configurable on the responsibility. It is a failure bound, not a normal animation delay. Binding or cancellation releases the wait exactly once. Tests must exercise a missing/throwing binder, including admission rejection, rather than assume every callback arrives.

A producer's throw/admission failure records a diagnostic and invokes its registered captured summary fallback once, then releases or fails its contribution according to the phase plan. No fallback reads current run state or awards points. Failure cannot indefinitely block the result; a terminal summary path is mandatory. A teardown cancels the action record/root, waits and descendants once, invalidates generation, detaches observers and removes completed records from the bounded registry. Observing a retained terminal handle immediately reports its captured terminal status; lookup after disposal reports unavailable. Retained status handles must not keep scene resources alive.

A scene cover follows the existing presentation-clock suspension policy; it does not create duplicate producers on resume. Actual exit/stage reset invalidates affected generations and prevents late updates replacing current scenery. The action registry has a finite configured record/registration cap and explicit rejection diagnostics; overflow uses the same captured-summary policy, never silent omission or input refusal. No model transaction depends on admission or visual completion. Restored state draws immediately without fabricating an in-flight action; history remains model data.

## Source audit, alternatives and delivery

Audit at `6009c59`: `src/play-presenter.ts:present` creates private root scopes indexed by a local numeric counter, while the model has its own committed action IDs. `actionScopes` is a Set of handles, not an action lookup. Its play effect ends after flights, then `announceScore` releases a reserved narration slot. `src/score-narration.ts` creates separate scopes for lines, tint holds and outcome; its `idle`/`presenting` are aggregate state, not per-action scoring completion. Contrary to the intake's shorthand, those narration scopes are not already children of the flight scope. `src/visual-events/scheduler.ts` supplies nested scopes, seal/status/observe, registered effects and bounded fallback, but no action/phase correlation or contribution reservation. `score.totalApplied` now supplies captured stage history (#1063), so the missing dependency is presentation ordering, not a second history store.

Starting at model commit would animate before scoring. A global narrator-idle callback delays an earlier graph behind later moves and loses action identity. Exposing presenter internals couples every consumer to legacy storage. Letting a completion observer attach required work after root settlement races result entry. Reserving contributions first and adapting current producers preserves independent composition and the scheduler's parent/child invariant. This proposal makes no claim that the new interfaces or migration are already built, nor that all legacy presenter timing is repaired by one change.

Separate deliveries: (1) shared reservation/status/barrier service tested with controlled producers and the real scheduler; (2) action-ID and completion adaptation of existing placement/scoring narration, including per-action tails and result completion; then #1087's independently composed graph controller consumes that seam. Each cites this merged contract. Do not combine graph rendering, all score migration and the registry into one PR.

Verification covers registration-order independence; no early parent settlement; multiple committed actions finishing flights out of order; each action's narration/tint completion; zero score; immediate and delayed binding; missing/throwing producer and admission/failure summary; late registration/lookup; ordered plot updates; later legal input; cancellation/reset/cover/exit; result cue after final graph; no deadlock; restoration without replay; bounded record/resource retention; duplicate callback/settlement; and no reads from live model in delayed handlers. Provide focused timing traces and actual game evidence for adapter adoption. This design supplies no browser acceptance or performance measurement.
